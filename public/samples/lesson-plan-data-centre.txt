Lesson plan: Data Centre Fundamentals (CDFA)

This is a sample lesson plan for a data centre fundamentals session. Upload it on a deck's
page, or copy its layout for your own plan: numbered sections, each with a time, then an
"Objective:" line. A "Slides:" line ties a section to slide numbers when the titles do not
match, for example "Slides: 4-5". Lines that are not in a section, like these, are ignored.

Session flow
1. Introduction and objectives (3 min)
   Objective: Welcome the group, introduce the course and set out what the session covers.
2. What a data centre is (8 min)
   Objective: Explain what a data centre does and why availability matters to the business it serves.
3. Key components (10 min)
   Objective: Walk through the data hall and name the main components: racks, servers, network, power distribution and cooling units.
4. Power: from the grid to the rack (15 min)
   Objective: Trace the power path from the utility feed to the rack, and show how the UPS and generators keep the load running when the utility fails.
5. Cooling (10 min)
   Objective: Explain how heat is removed from the data hall, including hot and cold aisle containment.
6. Tier levels and redundancy (10 min)
   Objective: Explain the four tier levels and what N, N+1 and 2N redundancy mean in practice.
7. Summary and questions (5 min)
   Objective: Recap the session and answer the group's questions.

Learning outcomes
- Describe what a data centre is for and why availability matters.
- Name the main components of a data hall and what each one does.
- Trace the power path from the utility feed to the rack, including the UPS and generators.
- Explain how cooling and containment keep IT equipment within its operating range.
- Explain the tier levels and the difference between N, N+1 and 2N redundancy.
